I built LeadFlow around the workflow a real brokerage follows: lead acquisition, pipeline management, client conversion and client document handling. From the start I made multi-tenancy a database-level rule, enforced in a Mongoose plugin from the signed-in user's token, so every brokerage sees only its own data, and I separated the four roles: platform admin, brokerage admin, advisor and client. On top of that I added the features that make the workflow useful: a live pipeline, document status tracking with background checking, email templates linked to pipeline stages, and tasks created when a lead enters a stage. For the problems that matter in practice, duplicate submissions and two advisors editing the same lead at once, I built the protection into the backend (idempotent intake with duplicate detection, and a version check that returns a 409 to the slower update) instead of hiding them in the UI. I used Claude during development, and all prompts are logged in PROMPTS.md.

Weak spots: I did not load-test 500 leads per minute, and there is no per-brokerage rate limiting, so one tenant could flood the service. Document checking recovers on restart but has no durable queue, retries or dead-letter handling. Failed emails are recorded but not retried, and delivery is unverified because the sender domain isn't verified. The webhook has no authentication, tasks are limited to one per stage, events missed while offline aren't replayed, and Render's free tier cold-starts. Next I would add per-brokerage webhook secrets, a BullMQ/Redis queue with retries and a dead-letter queue, per-tenant rate limits, a 500+/minute load test, and a verified sending domain.
